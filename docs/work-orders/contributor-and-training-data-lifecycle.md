## B. What the work actually looks like

### B1. Enrich the role dataset

For **every title in the verified snapshot**, capture its role family, posting status and date, advertised duties, prerequisites, likely entry route, typical input, action, work product, review standard, tools or environment, time and pay basis, and evidence status. Distinguish a role title, a qualification, and an actual project. Where multiple titles appear to share a workflow, link them to one family or project pattern and record what meaningfully differs. Preserve role-specific licenses, tools, subject matter, and restrictions.

Build one profile for each of the **14 families**. Each should answer what the contributor is asked to do, what they hand back, what would make the work acceptable, and how much is known about the actual workday. Include the most informative public role examples, plus documented variations within the family.

### B2. Walk through five work sessions in detail

Choose **Generalist, FP&A or Finance Expert, Software Engineering or DevOps, one clinical or legal specialty, and one multilingual role**. These cover different mixes of comparison, fact-checking, specialist judgment, testing, and language review. For each, construct a realistic session with these stages:

1. **Start the session.** Check the queue, project instructions, eligibility, pay display, task availability, and deadlines where documented.
2. **Set up the task.** Read the instructions and inputs. Identify the expected format, permitted tools, time limits, and how to handle unclear or conflicting evidence.
3. **Do the work.** Show the sequence of concrete actions and decisions, including any research, calculations, code execution, language checks, or rubric scoring. Describe what takes judgment and what can be verified mechanically.
4. **Submit and review.** Show the expected deliverable, quality checks, time reporting, and any documented feedback, rejection, or rework. Where available, trace the path from submission to payable work.
5. **Explain the variation.** Identify what may change by project, customer, specialization, or date. Make clear which details came from actual observations and which belong to the original illustration.

For the finance and coding walkthroughs, add a short synthetic task with an input, an example output, and two failure modes. Keep the walkthroughs usable by someone deciding which track they would enjoy and which skills they need to practice.

### B3. Establish what a workday can reasonably be estimated to cost or earn

Compare advertised hourly maxima with observed assignment rates only when a source provides context. Track unit of pay, onboarding or qualification time, paid task time, research or rework, waiting time, project gaps, and any minimums or limits that can be verified. If there is enough evidence, calculate an illustrative effective hourly rate from paid amounts divided by total time spent. Label its assumptions and keep isolated reports separate from platform-wide data. Leave frequency, hours, and typical earnings unknown where evidence is thin.

### B4. Apply the same evidence standard to every role

Begin with current official postings, help pages, and platform terms. Seek recent dated first-person accounts and independent reporting across several role families. Tag every source by role, timeframe, location when relevant, and whether the person was an applicant, contributor, or recruiter. Check promotional incentives and look for conflicting accounts. Keep selection details and day-to-day claims separate. Label them **officially stated**, **independently reported**, **contributor-reported**, **illustrative**, or **unknown**. A public posting supports advertised responsibilities. It leaves actual task frequency and queue conditions open unless additional evidence establishes them.

### C2. Compare role work products

Build the role-family crosswalk around who writes the task, who supplies the reference, who compares outputs, who checks correctness, and who releases the final record. For each role family, give one source-linked example of the published work and one clearly labeled interpretation of the handoff. Concentrate detailed examples on three distinct contributions.

- **Generalist** produces a preference judgment and a concise explanation. Record ties, both-bad results, unsupported claims, and genuine uncertainty.
- **Finance** supplies a reference answer and a grading rubric. Separate arithmetic and reconciliation checks from judgment about assumptions or the usefulness of a recommendation.
- **Engineering** defines a reproducible failure and tests the proposed fix. Record environment version, expected behavior, reproduction steps, and regressions.

### C3. Reconstruct the workflow and package the data

Design a small canonical record containing stable task and version IDs, scenario group, source and rights metadata, prompt, input manifest, expected output, and split. Keep candidate responses, reviewer judgments, adjudications, automated checks, and run results in linked records. Record who or what generated each output, with tool and model version when applicable. Create separate export views for demonstrations, preference pairs, and benchmark cases. Keep answer keys, hidden checks, and evaluator-only information out of the model’s held-out input.

### C4. Build one synthetic Boatable case pack

Use a fictional marine service desk. Create **12 original cases** across quote reconciliation, scope changes, and approval or scheduling conflicts. Assign six to development and six to held-out evaluation, with two cases of each type in each split. Group by input bundle and scenario template before splitting so paraphrases and near-duplicates stay together. Fix the held-out split before tuning prompts or choosing examples.

For each case, produce a task brief, source records, a reference deliverable, a plausible flawed candidate answer, and a criterion-by-criterion rubric. Include a deterministic check wherever the case supports one. A quote case can check arithmetic, whether the customer approved each change, and whether every final line item traces to the supplied record. A model that invents an approval or bills an unapproved change fails that criterion regardless of prose quality.

Pilot the rubric on three development cases before freezing it. When a second reviewer is available, have them score the same cases independently and record agreement and disagreements by criterion. Otherwise, run a separate review pass and label it as single-reviewer verification. Resolve or flag disagreements with a written reason and preserve the original decisions. Include an explicit invalid-record state for missing evidence or ambiguous task instructions.

Use synthetic records and public materials whose terms permit the intended use. Exclude private records, contributor-platform assignments, and live screening materials.

### C5. Demonstrate where the data goes

- **Supervised fine-tuning** packages an instruction and the reference response as a demonstration. Show the export, describe the objective, and identify which fields the model sees.
- **Preference learning** packages an instruction with preferred and rejected responses and keeps the human judgment in the audit trail. Explain how a reward-model workflow or direct preference optimization could consume the pair.
- **Verifier-based reinforcement learning** packages a task and an executable or criterion-based scoring function. Show what a run receives as feedback and demonstrate how a plausible shortcut fails the test.
- **Evaluation** runs a frozen model or agent on held-out tasks and records the resulting artifacts, scores, and failures. Keep evaluation results separate from data used to improve the tested configuration.

Run a local scoring harness on the reference answers and intentionally flawed answers first. Require each reference to pass the applicable deterministic checks and each seeded material error to trigger its intended failure. If a model is available in the existing environment, compare a baseline prompt with a prompt using development examples. Keep the model, test tasks, evaluator, and other material settings fixed. Report counts and per-case results for arithmetic, evidence traceability, and policy compliance. Classify this as an inference-time experiment. A weight-update run is an optional follow-on if suitable compute and enough training examples are already available.

## Completion criteria

- The company brief answers who operates DataAnnotation, what buyers pay for, and which customer or model connections are documented. It separates public facts from inferred or private handoffs.
- The selection map covers the path from application to paid work and ongoing access. Each transition has a source or a specific unknown.
- The role register accounts for every title in the reconciled snapshot. All 14 families have profiles and five tracks have concrete, evidence-labeled work-session walkthroughs.
- The findings distinguish advertised rates, documented pay practices, observed experience, and any modeled effective pay. They identify which work patterns and preparation matter most to Product Pat.
- Three public case traces identify the source evidence and any missing handoff. Each synthetic case has a stable ID, source manifest, split, reference, rubric, and reviewer status. Training demonstrations, preferences, and held-out evaluation records are separable.
- Deterministic checks pass the reference answers and catch the seeded errors. The report includes reproducible instructions, per-case results or a documented execution gap, and limits on any conclusions from the small sample.

Start the synthetic work with one runnable case, then expand to 12 once the reference and flawed controls behave as intended. The core experiment needs no new paid infrastructure.
