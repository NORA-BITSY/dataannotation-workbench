# First paid week

Before accepting each task, read the project-specific instructions and the displayed rate, pay basis and time reporting rules. Note what qualifies as billable preparation or rework and what a completed submission must include. The terms make payment conditional on satisfactory work. The FAQ describes varying access and payment mechanics. Neither establishes the rate, hours or payout date for a particular task. [Official terms](https://app.dataannotation.tech/contract/280) · [FAQ](https://www.dataannotation.tech/faqs)

## Each session

1. Check the queue briefly. Choose a task you can complete within its actual constraints. Record the category, rate, billing basis and time privately without retaining project content.
2. Before starting, list the requested deliverables and the allowed resources. On an hourly project, follow the stated rules for logging time. On per-task work, record paid units and time separately.
3. Before submitting, test the most material claim or action against the supplied evidence. Confirm the requested output, constraints and format. If feedback is visible, record the category of the issue and one process change without copying confidential text.
4. Keep estimated, approved and cleared amounts separate. Log unpaid prep, queue checks or rework only in your personal tracker as invested time. Use the platform's account support for a specific pay or access issue when available. [Tracker template](../../data/earnings/personal-tracker-template.csv)

## End-of-week review

Compare hours invested, paid share, approved pay per invested hour, and actual cash received. Keep the period of work separate from the date cash clears. Look for task types with good effective pay and a consistently verifiable quality bar. Reduce time on speculative qualifications or empty queues when they crowd out useful work. Keep an explicit zero-work week rather than estimating missed income. Use the [earnings model guide](earnings-model.md) for the definitions and keep actual task information outside Git.
